# Messaging Framework

## The Hierarchy

Build messaging top-down. Each level must be consistent with the one above it.
Every piece of copy written — from homepage hero to 404 error message — should be
traceable back to the brand promise.

### Level 1: Category Claim
What space do you own or are creating? One phrase. This is the macro frame.
- Format: "[Brand] is [category] for [audience]"
- Must use Rung 4-5 language from the Specificity Ladder
- Avoid: any framing an incumbent could adopt without contradiction

### Level 2: Brand Promise
The single most important guarantee. Must be specific and falsifiable.
The test: could a customer call you a liar if you fail to deliver this?

- Bad: "We make finance easier" (unfalsifiable — what's easier? compared to what?)
- Bad: "We're the most trusted [X]" (no one promises to be the least trusted)
- Good: "Your AP team never touches a routine invoice" (falsifiable, specific, ownable)

### Level 3: Proof Points
Three concrete reasons the promise is believable. Each must be demonstrable.
Format: [Specific fact/feature] → [What it means for the customer]

The proof point has two parts and both must be present. The fact alone is a spec sheet.
The meaning alone is marketing copy. Together they're a trust-building statement.

### Level 4: Tagline
Compressed brand promise. Maximum 8 words. Must work without context.
Tests: (1) Does it work on a billboard with no logo? (2) Could only you say this?
(3) Does it imply the benefit without describing the product?

### Level 5: Voice
How everything is said. Applies to every piece of copy in existence — not just marketing.
Your 404 error page, your invoice email, your onboarding screen, your careers page.
Voice is the system. Tone is how voice adapts to context.

## The Voice Architecture

### Four Dimensions, Five-Point Scale

Establish voice by scoring four dimensions. The score is not a constraint — it's an
anchor. Voice can stretch into adjacent territory when context demands it (a serious
brand can be funny; it just doesn't default to funny). The score defines the default.

**Dimension 1: Formal ↔ Casual**
| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional. Full sentences, no contractions, passive voice permitted. | Professional but human. Contractions allowed. Reads like a smart person wrote it. | Smart casual. Warm, direct. "We built something" over "we have developed a solution." | Conversational. Talks like a person thinks. Occasional aside or parenthetical. | Friend texting. Lowercase, abbreviations, personality over grammar. |

**Dimension 2: Reserved ↔ Enthusiastic**
| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Understated. Lets the product speak. "It works." | Quietly confident. "We think you'll notice the difference." | Warm. "We're glad you're here." | Energetic. "This changes everything." | Hyperbolic. "THE MOST INCREDIBLE THING EVER." |

**Dimension 3: Serious ↔ Humorous**
| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zero humor. Enterprise security, medical, legal. | Dry wit permitted. One unexpected line allowed per page. | Occasional lightness. Friendly without jokes. | Humor-forward. Copy makes people smile. | Comedy brand. The humor IS the product. |

**Dimension 4: Technical ↔ Accessible**
| 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Developer docs. Exact terminology. No translation. | Technical but clear. Respects the audience's intelligence. | Smart layperson. Explains without condescending. | Non-technical. Benefits over mechanisms. | Explain-like-I'm-5. Metaphors everywhere. |

## Tagline Generation Framework

Generate across ALL five strategic angles. The first tagline is usually the obvious one.
The best is usually the fourth or fifth. Never deliver one option.

**Angle 1: Outcome-first** — Lead with what the customer gets
> "AP that runs itself." / "Ship in the time it used to take to plan."

**Angle 2: Enemy-first** — Name the problem you're ending
> "Close the books, not tickets." / "Stop managing your AP. Start doing finance."

**Angle 3: Identity-first** — Tell customers who they become with you
> "For CFOs who build, not babysit." / "For teams that care about craft."

**Angle 4: Category-first** — Claim the space
> "The end of manual AP." / "Autonomous accounts payable."

**Angle 5: Provocation** — Challenge the status quo belief
> "Your AP team is too good for data entry." / "Routine invoices should handle themselves."

For each tagline delivered, specify:
- Strategic angle used
- Best context: homepage hero / ad headline / email subject / social bio / conference badge
- Confidence rating: **Strong** (own it) / **Solid** (test it) / **Worth testing** (has potential, needs proof)
- Why it works or why it might not: one honest sentence

## The Messaging Stress Tests

Before finalizing any key message, run these:

### The Opposite Test
Flip the claim. Would any legitimate competitor claim the opposite?
- "We're reliable" → opposite is "we're unreliable" → no competitor claims this → worthless claim
- "AP that runs itself" → opposite is "AP that requires your team" → competitors claim this implicitly → real differentiation

### The 3am Test
Would your best customer quote this to a colleague at 3am (metaphorically)? People
only repeat things that feel true AND feel worth repeating. A message that's true
but boring doesn't spread. A message that's catchy but not true doesn't stick.

### The Boring Room Test
Say the message in a room of smart people who have no prior relationship with your brand.
Is it interesting enough that at least one person asks a question? If everyone nods
politely and moves on, the message has no hook.

## Word Banks

### Power Words (use these)
Built, crafted, engineered, designed, ships, closes, runs, owns, controls, sees, knows,
moves, scales, eliminates, automates, handles, replaces, ends, removes, finishes,
returns, recovers, restores, delivers, earns, makes possible

### Banned Words (brand poison — never use)
synergy, best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge, next-generation, disruptive,
innovative (unless proven), seamless, frictionless, empower, enable, solution,
platform (as a differentiator — fine as a category noun), holistic, end-to-end
(unless literal), AI-powered (as differentiator — everyone says this),
game-changing, transformative (unless you prove the transformation),
passionate (show it, don't say it), dedicated (table stakes), committed (table stakes),
proud (no one cares that you're proud)

### Replacement Table
| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Our solution enables..." | "You can now..." |
| "Seamless integration" | "Works with [tool] in one click" |
| "AI-powered automation" | "Invoices match themselves — 99.2% accuracy" |
| "Best-in-class" | [cite the specific proof] |
| "We're passionate about X" | [show a specific decision you made because of that passion] |
| "We're proud to announce" | [just announce it] |
| "Our platform" | "[product name]" or "the tool" |
| "Innovative approach" | [name the actual innovation] |
| "World-class team" | [name who's on the team and why they're exceptional] |
| "We empower [audience]" | "[Audience] can now [specific thing]" |

## Copy By Context

### Homepage Hero (cold traffic, maximum stakes)
Goal: One thought. One action. Nothing else.
Structure: Claim → Proof → CTA
Word count: Headline ≤8 words, subheadline ≤20 words, CTA ≤4 words
Test: Passes the Screenshot Test and the "So What" Test simultaneously

### Ad Copy (paid channels, zero context)
Goal: Stop the scroll. Create recognition of a problem or desire.
Structure: Hook (1 sentence) → Proof (1-2 sentences) → CTA
B2B: Lead with the problem your audience hates most. Don't explain the product.
B2C: Lead with the identity the audience wants. Show the person they become.

### Email Subject Lines
Formula bank:
- [Number] [thing] that [outcome]: "3 invoices your team should never touch again"
- The [thing] you [forgot/didn't know/never noticed]: "The AP workflow costing you 20 hrs/month"
- [Name], [specific observation]: (personalization + insight)
- [Question your audience asks internally]: "Why does AP take so long?"
- [Provocative statement]: "Your AP team is doing data entry. Stop."

### Onboarding Copy
Goal: Get to first value as fast as possible. Celebrate the milestone.
Tone: Always one degree warmer than your baseline brand voice. Users are vulnerable here.
Never: "Complete your profile." (Chore language.)
Always: "Two minutes and you're ready." (Outcome language.)

### Error Messages
The most underbought copy in most products. Error messages are a brand moment.
Formula: [What happened] + [Why (if helpful)] + [What to do next]
Voice: Maintain brand voice even here. A Jester brand's 404 can be funny.
A Sage brand's 404 should be clear and helpful, never condescending.
Never: "An error occurred." / "Something went wrong." (Tells user nothing.)
Always: "We couldn't find that page — it may have moved. [Search] or [go home]."
