# Example: Vaultly — B2B SaaS Brand Build

This example shows the Branding Wizard working through a complete brand build for a B2B SaaS company.

---

## User Input
> "I'm building Vaultly — a B2B SaaS for finance teams that automates accounts payable. Seed stage, $500k ARR, targeting mid-market CFOs. Our brand currently looks like every other fintech — navy blue, 'efficiency' messaging, stock photos of handshakes. We hate it. Competitors: Tipalti, Bill.com. Admired brands: Stripe, Linear. We want to feel modern, trustworthy but not stuffy, built for people who care about craft."

---

## Brand DNA Output

### Archetype Diagnosis
**Primary: Creator (70%)** — Craft-obsessed, built-to-last, shows the work behind the work. The brand itself is a product of careful design decisions.

**Secondary: Sage (30%)** — Intelligent, precise, data-informed. The brand earns trust through demonstrated competence, not claims.

**What this means:** Vaultly brands like a design tool that happens to do finance. Think Figma's intentionality applied to AP workflows. Every touchpoint signals "someone who gives a damn built this." The intelligence is shown, not told — clean data presentation, precise interactions, no wasted elements.

### Positioning

**Category Claim:** Vaultly is autonomous accounts payable for finance teams that care about craft.

**Against / For / Beyond:**
| Dimension | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Against** | Not another dashboard that makes CFOs feel like data analysts. Not the corporate blue rectangle. |
| **For** | For finance teams who'd rather close the books than manage a tool. For CFOs who notice good design. |
| **Beyond** | AP that runs itself — finance people doing finance, not data entry. |

**Positioning Statement:** For mid-market finance leaders who waste 20+ hours weekly on manual AP, Vaultly is the autonomous accounts payable platform that processes invoices without human intervention — unlike Tipalti and Bill.com which digitized the manual process instead of eliminating it.

### Messaging

**Brand Promise:** Your AP closes itself — no human touches a routine invoice.

**Proof Points:**
1. AI-matched invoices with 99.2% accuracy → You stop manually coding line items
2. Auto-approval for recurring vendors under set thresholds → Your team stops rubber-stamping
3. Real-time cash flow visibility → You see exactly where your money is, not where it was yesterday

**Tagline Options:**
1. "AP that runs itself." — Outcome-first. Works everywhere. (Strong)
2. "Close the books, not tickets." — Enemy-first. Great for ads targeting frustrated teams. (Solid)
3. "Built for CFOs who build." — Identity-first. Strong for brand-level, weaker for product marketing. (Worth testing)
4. "The end of manual AP." — Category-first. Bold claim. Works for launch. (Strong)
5. "Finance, meet autopilot." — Metaphor. Accessible, slightly consumer-y. (Worth testing)

**Recommended:** "AP that runs itself." — Three words. Passes the billboard test. Implies both the automation and the trust. Competitors can't say it because their products still require manual steps.

**Voice:**
- Formal(2) ↔ Casual — Professional but readable. Never stuffy.
- Reserved(2) ↔ Enthusiastic — Quietly confident. Let the product speak.
- Serious(3) ↔ Humorous — Dry wit allowed. Never silly. Finance people appreciate deadpan.
- Technical(2) ↔ Accessible — Technical enough to earn respect, clear enough to not exclude.

### Visual Direction

**Scores:** Minimal(2) / Cool(4) / Serious(3) / Novel(3) / Literal(2)

**Color System:**
| Role | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | #0A0F1E | Deep space navy — backgrounds, headers. Authority without corporate staleness. |
| Secondary | #6366F1 | Electric indigo — the "craft" signal. Interactive elements, accents. Nods to Linear/Figma. |
| Accent | #22D3EE | Cyan — CTAs, success states, data highlights. Used sparingly. |
| Background | #FFFFFF | Primary surfaces in light mode |
| Surface | #F8FAFC | Subtle surface differentiation |
| Text primary | #0F172A | Near-black for body copy |
| Text secondary | #64748B | Muted for secondary information |

**Typography:**
- Headlines: Inter — tight tracking (-0.02em at display sizes), Bold/Semibold
- Body: Inter — 16px, 1.6 line-height, Regular
- Data/Mono: JetBrains Mono — financial figures, invoice numbers, technical content

**Imagery:**
- Product screenshots as hero imagery — clean, actual UI, no mockup frames
- No stock photography. Zero handshakes. Zero smiling-at-laptop.
- Illustration: minimal, geometric, data-visualization-inspired
- Photography (if ever used): real team, real office, documentary style — not posed

**Layout:**
- 12-column grid, generous whitespace (density matches Linear, not Salesforce)
- Cards with subtle borders, not drop shadows
- Information hierarchy enforced through type size and weight, not decoration

**References:**
1. **Linear** — Steal: product-as-hero, dark mode craft, precise spacing. Skip: ultra-dark as default — finance needs light mode primary.
2. **Stripe** — Steal: developer-grade documentation quality applied to all content, gradient accents used sparingly. Skip: the rainbow — too playful for AP.
3. **Vercel** — Steal: the confidence of letting the product speak, minimal marketing fluff. Skip: the developer-only aesthetic — CFOs need warmer onboarding.

---

## Why This Works

1. **Differentiation is immediate.** Open Vaultly next to Bill.com or Tipalti — you can tell in 2 seconds this is a different kind of company.
2. **The archetype combo (Creator + Sage) maps to the target audience.** Mid-market CFOs who admire Stripe are people who notice craft and respect intelligence. This brand speaks their language.
3. **"AP that runs itself" is both a tagline and a product promise.** It's falsifiable — if the product doesn't deliver, the brand breaks. That constraint keeps everyone honest.
4. **The visual direction has clear rules.** A junior designer can make decisions without asking "does this feel on-brand?" because the specs are concrete enough to evaluate against.
